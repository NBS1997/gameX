# Tide / Pull

**Genre:** Physics-based one-button arcade.

**Hook:** You're the moon. Pull the tide in, push it out. A single button, a thousand wrecks.

**Target player:** Pick-up-and-play arcade fans; people who liked *Downwell* or *Vampire Survivors*' "easy to learn" surface; mobile-style players who actually want skill expression.

**Core mechanic:** Top-down view of a small bay. Press-and-hold pulls water *toward* the cursor; releasing lets it rush back. The water has weight and momentum. Boats, debris, and (later) creatures drift on the surface. Each level is a single screen with a goal — guide a boat to harbour, drown a fire, deliver flotsam to the beachcomber, save a swimmer — using only tide control. Failure states are gentle (the level resets in 2 seconds, no death animation, no shame).

**Why now:** *Spilled!* hitting big in 2025/2026 confirmed the appetite for one-screen, gentle, water-themed arcade games, and one-button design is having a small renaissance because it streams and clips well.

**Vertical slice:** Six levels, one mechanic (the pull/release), a basic fluid sim (cellular-automaton water on a coarse grid is fine), a soft palette, and a single ambient track. Built in Pixi.js or plain WebGL on Canvas. Windows-runnable trivially as a browser game.

**Comparable titles:**
- *Spilled!* (Lente Cuenen, 2025) — gentle one-screen water game; Tide / Pull is the "your input is the physics" cousin.
- *Downwell* (Moppin, 2015) — one-button mastery reference.
- *Loop Hero* (Four Quarters, 2021) — not gameplay-similar, but the "minimal input, deep system" framing is the same elevator pitch.

**Risks:**
- Cheap fluid sim that *feels good* is the entire game and is the riskiest single tech bet here.
- Six "interesting" levels from one mechanic is a real design challenge — risk of the fourth and fifth levels feeling redundant.
- "One-button" is a marketing hook that can hide a thin game; the depth has to come from level design.

## How this premise was chosen

The user invoked `/concept-pitch` with no nudge, asking the team to propose anything. The creative director returned five genre-distinct candidates: *Marginalia* (document-examination puzzle), *Keysmith* (typing action), *Lost & Found* (cosy bureaucracy sim), *Tide / Pull* (one-button physics arcade), and *The Last Bus* (short narrative walking sim). The director's own lean was towards *Marginalia* on tech-simplicity and clip-friendliness grounds, but framed the pick as "what do you want to spend two weeks living inside" rather than market fit. The user picked Candidate 4, *Tide / Pull*, on instinct ("sounds fun"). No refinement round was run and nothing from the original director memo was cut — the candidate is being carried forward verbatim into the council phase. The genuine open questions for the council to resolve are the fluid-sim approach (the riskiest tech bet), the differentiator from *Spilled!*, and how six levels from one mechanic stay non-redundant.
